## Time-coded narration

### 0:00–0:20 | Slide 1 — Yumma

A phone rings. A message creates urgency. Someone must decide quickly, often without the context that could keep them safe. Yumma is a personal life intelligence AI agent that brings a person's own words back when pressure rises — while leaving the decision with them.

### 0:20–0:40 | Slide 2 — The problem

One pressured decision can affect money, health, family, access and daily independence. Yet digital support is fragmented across institutions and products. Yumma addresses a focused gap: a person's own calm-state commitment is often missing at the moment they need it.

### 0:40–1:00 | Slide 3 — What is different

Banks apply bank rules. Family monitoring alerts someone else. General assistants answer questions. Yumma combines consented personal commitments, wider life-impact reasoning and independent safety controls. We make this combination testable; it must earn trust in real use.

### 1:00–1:25 | Slide 4 — Why now

The UAE is a practical first market. A 2024 UAE study reported that 56 percent of survey respondents encountered a scam attempt at least monthly; 27 percent of respondents reported losing money, with an average loss of 8,058 dirhams. These are study findings, not a prediction of Yumma's impact. They show why a timely pause matters.

### 1:25–1:50 | Slide 5 — First market

We begin with UAE families supporting an older adult or relative who needs help navigating money decisions. Our first pilot hypothesis is 20 to 30 families. A year-one ambition of 1,000 families is a planning target, not proven traction. Expansion follows evidence.

### 1:50–2:20 | Slide 6 — The innovation

Four mechanisms work together. The Calm-State Contract records a person's own commitment while they feel comfortable making it. The Personal Wisdom Graph holds permissioned context such as values and boundaries. The Life Ripple Engine connects choices to wider consequences. Mother's Second Thought creates a warm, structured pause. A commitment guides conversation; it never grants authority to act.

### 2:20–2:40 | Slide 7 — The experience

The intended experience is a warm, voice-led presence, with voice, text or quiet mode chosen by the person. The emerald-and-gold Abaya hologram is a visual concept; mobile implementation and a live 3D avatar are not complete. The visual presence creates no authority. Permissions remain visible and revocable.

### 2:40–3:10 | Slide 8 — Salma's journey

In this synthetic example, Salma records: “I never move money because someone calls or messages me.” Later, a caller claims to be from her bank, asks for an OTP and pressures her to transfer 18,000 dirhams to a new beneficiary. Yumma recalls her words, identifies the pressure signals and recommends independent verification. Salma decides. No real bank or transaction is connected.

### 3:10–3:35 | Slide 9 — Trust and authority

Trust cannot depend on personality. Interpretation is separated from policy and authority. Consent and deterministic safety rules govern what may happen. Yumma does not move money, change beneficiaries, freeze accounts, recommend investments, diagnose, prescribe or change treatment. Qualified professionals retain regulated decisions; the person remains in control.

### 3:35–4:15 | Slide 10 — What works today

Today, a local browser interface connects to a Node.js agent runtime. It demonstrates profile-scoped local memory, consent, expiry and trigger checks, Calm-State Contract recall, deterministic risk assessment, protection orchestration, four synthetic scenarios, temporary sessions and local audit records. The model adapter exists, but no live provider is configured. There is no live bank integration or external financial action. Android validation, production identity, encrypted storage assurance, independent security review, user research and a live 3D hologram remain to be proven.

### 4:15–4:40 | Slide 11 — Evidence

The current local verification passed 64 automated Node.js tests and all four synthetic judge scenarios. HTTP smoke checks and the full verification command also passed. These are engineering checks on synthetic data, not proof of real-world fraud accuracy, production readiness, security assurance or user outcomes. Next: Android device validation, live-model evaluation, the required narrated agent demo, family feedback and independent review.

### 4:40–5:05 | Slide 12 — Business model

Affordability comes first. Essential protection should remain free. A five-dirham monthly family contribution is a hypothesis, not a validated price. Banks, insurers, employers, NGOs or foundations may sponsor access, but no commitment is secured. We will measure operating and safeguarding costs before setting a sustainable price.

### 5:05–5:25 | Slide 13 — Go to market

We start with 20 to 30 families. We measure activation, consent, retention, promise-resurfacing accuracy, false alarms, missed risks and trust. Only after proving the first journey do we pursue care organisations, accessibility communities, banks, insurers and family-support networks.

### 5:25–5:50 | Slides 14–15 — Founder and close

My background spans more than 25 years in banking, technology and transformation. I am building Yumma around a simple belief: care should notice the moment that matters, ask the gentle question and protect independence — never replace judgement. Yumma helps people remember what matters, see wider consequences and choose the next safe step. Thank you.
